# LootBox design

LootBox is a provisional name. This is an independent Build 42 client mod, with executable code exclusively in `media/lua/client`. A multiplayer host may enable the mod through the normal mod list, but installs no LootBox server logic. Native game actions remain authoritative. It has no dependency on LuaBuddy or any of the four reference mods.

## One language for the player

A category is a reusable selection of items. Native categories come from the current game's item catalog, including installed mods; no fixed category mapping compresses or drops them. A custom category contains item types, native categories, and other custom categories. Includes mean union. Cycles are refused. Stable IDs are independent of translated names.

Container labels describe what belongs there: `item:<id>:<type>` for one item, `type:<fullType>` for a kind, custom and native categories, and `rest:` for anything else (`Labels`). `state.storage[key]` holds a vehicle part's name and the fill order (`first` or `last`); a fixture's name is the game's own `ItemContainer:getCustomName()`, remembered for when it is out of sight. Labels, name and order move together when a key changes. Item labels add a particular instance to categories; adding an item type to a custom category affects every instance instead. Keep protects an instance from automated movement and deletion; keeping a kind (`state.keepTypes`) holds the carried ones and blocks discarding any, while allowing items in transit, eating, drinking and pouring. The loot list describes what the player wants to find, by category, kind or one marked item, and needs no tagging of world containers; a marked item leaves the list once it is carried. These are different player intentions with separate controls.

## One job model

Every operation is a source scope, an item selection, and a destination. Loot brings matching items to carried storage. Move sends them to a chosen bag or container, a floor location, the remembered or another observed vehicle, labeled storage, or nearby containers that already hold the same kind. Clean hauls them to real trash cans and batches native deletion. The same selection supports an item type, multiple native or custom categories, or all unprotected items. Source scopes are what is carried, one open container or all of them, within reach, the current room or building, a rectangle on one floor, and containers with chosen labels; including carried contents and recursing into bags are explicit options.

Ordinary scopes take a finite source snapshot. A building or a marked area becomes a list of sections of at most 8 by 8 tiles. What is loaded and has been seen is read when planning, up to a fixed number of tiles, so the plan shows real totals; `Survey` keeps the rest and always gives the job the nearest one. It never calls `BuildingDef.getSquares()`. Loaded sections can be inspected without forcing the character onto an obstructed room center; native container approaches still govern transfers. Unloaded sections use normal coordinate or loaded-frontier walking and a finite streaming wait, so a saved area works from elsewhere. A current-floor frontier can lead toward distant stairs before the desired floor loads. Permission, path, geometry, and streaming failures remain unsearched rather than becoming empty rooms.

Jobs revalidate each native action against live state: `Jobs:admit` holds every rule, once, just before `Native:begin`, and `Jobs:reach` is the one way to walk to a container, a tile or stored gear. Source anchors retain location and fixture, bag, or keyed-vehicle identity so a capacity-limited delivery can return after source chunks unload. Floor coordinates resolve to the current native square, and immutable item IDs and types prevent substituting another item or source. The runner does not read unloaded inventories, assume another player's inventory remains unchanged, or wait forever for future loot respawns. Cancel stops owned work and restores temporary vehicle access where native actions permit restoration.

## Routing and capacity

Explicit destinations win. Labeled storage is ranked by `Storage` once for jobs, tooltips, menus and Put Away. Closeness comes first: the very item (0), its kind or a category given to that copy (1), a category (2), anything else (3, never keys). Within a closeness, storage filled first leads and storage filled last, or a carried bag labelled by category, trails; then where the same thing already is, unless the label names the item or its kind, then a label for one of the player's categories before one of the game's, then distance. Shared capacity is reserved across the planned batch. An item moves on only to storage meant for it more closely (`placesFor`), and only where there is room, so nothing is passed between equals. On the player's own bags only closeness 0 and 1 make a place, and loot in a bag labelled by category waits half a step for storage that stays put. `destination.near` keeps a job to storage within range of where it started, and `near.fixed` to storage that stays put; `request.only` limits a job to certain item IDs and is never saved as a preset. `AutoPutAway` keeps the IDs carried when leaving home in `state.trip`, adds what came back in the vehicle once it is carried, and starts a fixed, near job for the rest after three still, safe seconds. The runner rechecks actual capacity before transfer. When both ends are in reach an item goes straight across; otherwise each load makes one stop per destination, nearest first. Light items of one type travel up to twenty to an action, merged by the game's own transfer and confirmed one by one; what it leaves out is tried alone. A container or vehicle that unloads while its load is gathered is walked to before its room is judged. Vehicle storage prefers the trunk, then native seat/other storage; a locked vehicle that opens neither by itself nor with a key is unlocked once from the driver's seat. A full destination does not silently become a floor drop. Floor placement is an explicit destination.

Carried admission has two independent constraints. Native `hasRoomFor` and effective capacity govern each container; the main inventory's live `getCapacityWeight()` and `getEffectiveCapacity(player)` govern weighted mass across all carried destinations. Only the outermost directly carried bag applies equipped or fake-equipped reduction to nested contents. Attached unequipped bags use their native hotbar ratio. Shared preview reservations count positive mass changes across all bags, and runtime recomputes each pickup. A nonpositive rearrangement remains allowed while already overloaded. Condense chooses the target arrangement first (`Pack`: least carried weight, then the same kinds together, then fewest moves, in whole units so sums cannot drift) and then orders each item's single move to its target, parking one item in main inventory only where a swap blocks itself. A refused move is planned around and tried once more at the end. Carried bag destinations are independent of source traversal. `World:bags` answers which carried bags count, for Smart Load and Condense alike: worn, held and key rings, plus the two Condense settings. A load on its way elsewhere uses worn and held bags only. Feasible bags precede main inventory, those holding similar items first, then the lowest actual marginal mass, preserving stable order on ties. The normal HUD threshold is informational; native hard admission remains authoritative.

A can is remembered as `trash["x:y:z"] = capacity`; the game's own `getTrashReceptacle()` says what stands there. Cans are noticed, refreshed and forgotten on every seen tile within eight tiles of the player, and a plan also looks around on the loaded floors of the building and the ground. `World:bins()` returns the usable ones within 150 tiles of the player or of the place being cleaned. Beyond that nothing is offered from menus and an explicit plan says how far the nearest is. Safehouse permission filters without forgetting. The job uses the nearest can that takes the item; a bigger one wins only when the emptyings it saves outweigh the detour, at two seconds each. Travel is estimated, and completed walks improve the estimate. Each can is emptied before it is left; cans filled directly from a container, or standing in the place being cleaned, are emptied at the end. Rubbish already in a can is never taken out of it. Rubbish within reach of a can goes straight in. Items no can can hold are never picked up.

On arrival an unopened can is opened first, as the loot window would. One rule decides about its contents: if asking is on and it holds anything not yet approved, the job waits in `needs_review`; with asking off the contents are approved as they are. Protected contents always block the can. An empty can needs no question. Approvals last for the job, across pauses. Review tokens are fresh, and approval rechecks reach, protection and membership. A can that cannot be used is left out until Resume; when none is left the job pauses with the load in hand. An explored flag or item-handle change is not a server acknowledgment. Native empty-trash deletes the entire server bin and ignores client approval IDs, so client checks cannot make shared-bin deletion atomic. The UI states this limit. Multiplayer `TrashDeleteAll` must permit the action. Cleanup does not change respawn settings or guarantee respawn; the server owns that policy.

A different can on the same tile is simply the can there now; deletion depends on contents alone. Successful approach observations are recorded at the action boundary, including floor walks. A walk gets 60 seconds without progress and a ten-minute total ceiling; transfer, trash, and vehicle deadlines remain independent.

## Storage

Storage is keyed `world:x:y:z:…`, `vehicle:…`, `bag:…` or `corpse:…`; the player's own inventory, corpses and trash cans are not labelled. A fixture's name is the game's `setCustomName`, which every player sees and which a safehouse the player may not use refuses; a bag keeps the name the game's own menu gives it; names are trimmed, stripped of control characters and at most 28 characters. The fill order is As usual, First or Last. A container for anything else is offered only what nothing else takes, and never a key. A labelled bag is for what its labels say unless the item is only passing through, and a place for one item keeps nothing else out. An item's mark follows it to a new ID, and is dropped when no container or list names it.

Tooltips and buttons reuse one answer (`glance`) for up to a second, and every label change clears it. `home()` names the place a job would use: within 20 tiles first, then any in sight, then one out of sight, and says when every one in sight is full. Put Away keeps to storage within 20 tiles of where it starts (`near`); automatic put-away also only to storage that stays put (`near.fixed`). Home, for automatic put-away, is such storage within 15 tiles to arrive and 25 to leave; `state.trip` holds the character's name and the IDs carried, and in the vehicle, on leaving. Movement, menus, drags, text entry, crafting windows, LootBox's windows and mouse presses put a start off and danger postpones it; anything the player does stops a running one, and turning the feature off forgets the trip.

## Interface

A row exists only when choosing it would do something to what was clicked, at that moment. `Menus` builds from facts: what is selected, where it is, what is carried, whether a container is empty, locked or unexplored, whether labels, trash cans or the remembered vehicle are known, and whether the player is indoors. There are no disabled rows and no generic entries. Submenus are created by their first row, so an empty one cannot exist; a single choice is one row, except deletion, which always stays one level down. Every row and submenu has a native icon.

`Facts` answers these questions without discovery, disk access or world scans. `World:lookForBins` records trash cans within sight when the player changes tile, saved at most every 30 seconds, when the game saves, and at the end of play.

`ObjectMenu` gives a container icon the original options of its object. It builds the native world menu twice into the already open root menu, because native Java builders read the root's `instanceMap` and throw on a submenu: once for the bare floor and once for the object. Options the floor also offers are global and dropped, as are options whose arguments name another object on the tile. The icon, its NeatUI title and the empty space of its list share one path, followed by options other mods add to containers, then LootBox.

`UI` is the single way a click becomes work: it plans, says so if there is nothing to do, asks only when the player chose to be asked (or when carried items would be deleted), and starts. `App` owns job succession. Planning while a job exists uses a second runner. Starting that plan pauses the current job, and once its native action settles the new runner takes over and the previous job stays resumable. Eating, drinking and pouring borrow the player: the job pauses, then resumes only if it had been running.

`UIKit` is the whole visual vocabulary: one palette, quiet buttons, cards, a row list with icons, and native context menus for every choice, so options live in submenus instead of dropdowns. `Picker` is the only category and item chooser. `JobBar` is a strip shown when a job lasts or needs the player. Settings apply immediately.

## Trace

`Log` writes numbered interactions to a rotating local file. `Log.intent` opens one with the control, the wording, the player and the container or item involved; the number travels through `request.interaction` and `options.interaction` into the engine, which adds the plan, each operation with the state around it, pauses with their trigger, and the end. Describers never throw and are skipped when detail is off. `Log.guard` and `Log.fault` report a swallowed error once per site.

## Runtime module contract

`Categories` owns category IDs, graph validation and matching. `Store` owns local versioned persistence. `Catalog` reads installed native script items. `Survey` owns the sections of a building or area still to be searched. `Routing` owns travel estimates and the bounded observed travel cache. `Carry` owns contents-aware native bag and slot planning. `Recovery` owns durable whole-personal-item staging intents and observed restoration. `World` owns loaded source and destination discovery, source anchors, and static bin identity. `Native` owns native action submission and owned-walk observation; it stands beside a tile rather than on it, because the game will not walk into furniture. `Clock` is time for deadlines: a paused game does not use it up. `Jobs` owns finite planning, progressive walking, delivery, and transient bin approval. `Setups` validates portable requests and interface preferences. `App` owns per-local-player state and persistence APIs. `Menus` builds context requests. `UI` starts work; `MainWindow` holds the planner, loot list, categories, storage, settings and help pages, beside `Review`, `JobBar` and `AreaPicker`. `Labels` owns what a label can say and keeps a container's labels, name and order together. `Storage` gives `World` one ranking of labelled storage for jobs, menus, tooltips and Put Away. `StorageMenu` builds the container and item storage rows, and `StorageView` shows labels on container titles, icons and item tooltips and points the way with Show me. `AutoPutAway` puts away what the player brought home. `Pack` chooses where carried items belong and `Condense` orders the moves. `Opening` opens food and drink with the game's recipe, and `Vessels` holds the fetch, pour and put-back steps the fluid flows share. Every item job goes through the same App preview/start API. Eating, drinking, pouring and dispensers are `Flow`s of stock actions owned by `Act`, and find their results with `Items`; see [finding results](finding-results.md).

`App.catalog()` returns `{items={{fullType,name,categoryId}}, categories={{id,name,kind}}}`. Native category IDs are `native:<raw display category>` and missing categories use the item's native type category. Custom categories use `custom:<stable ID>`. Custom definitions are `{id,name,types={fullType,...},includes={categoryId,...}}`. `App.saveCategory(definition)` returns `id` or `nil,error`; `App.deleteCategory(id)` also removes every reference to it in this session. Saved documents are repaired entry by entry: an unreadable preference, preset, label or category costs only itself.

`App.get(playerIndex)` returns a per-player application. `app:preview(request)` returns a plan with `moves`, `skipped`, `warnings` and `request`; each display row has `itemName`, `from`, `to` and an optional `reason`. `app:start(plan,options)` starts the reviewed plan with optional per-job `{reviewBins=false}`. `app:pause()`, `app:resume()` and `app:cancel()` manage owned work. `app:status()` returns state, done, moved, cleared, deferred, total, reason and control availability. Requests have `{scope,area,sourceContainer,sourceContainers,sourceLabels,includeInventory,includeBags,excludeUnwanted,filter,destination}`, plus `only` (a set of item IDs), `title`, `shortcutIntent` and `interaction`, which are never saved. Scope is `inventory`, `container`, `lootPane`, `reach`, `room`, `building`, `area` or `labeled`. Filter is `{ids={categoryId,...},types={fullType,...},all=boolean}`. Destination is `{kind,ref,labels,mode,near}` with kind `inventory`, `floor`, `container`, `vehicle`, `labels`, `trash` or `matching`; `mode` is `displayed` or `nearby`, and `near` is `{x,y,range,fixed}`. Area is `{x1,y1,x2,y2,z}`.

`app:labels(target)` returns labels and `app:setLabels(target,ids)` persists them, where target is a container or a saved key, so storage out of sight can be edited. `app:storageDetails(target)` and `app:setStorage(target,{name,order})` manage name and order; `app:setItemPlace(item,target,on)` gives one item one place; `app:setItemWanted(item,on)` marks one item for the loot list until it is carried; `app:keepType(fullType,on)` keeps a kind. `app:itemLabels(item)` and `app:setItemLabels(item,ids)` manage instance memberships; `app:protect(item,value)` changes Keep. `app:rememberVehicle(vehicle)` sets the hauling vehicle. `app:knownVehicles()` returns observed vehicles. `app:currentContainer()` returns the selected native loot container. Persistence is local; sharing labels between players is outside the client-only contract.

`app:reviewBin()` returns a transient review while the job waits in `needs_review`. `app:approveBin(review,options)` and `app:rejectBin(review)` accept only that current token. Public display mutations do not extend its private item approval. Jobs run on a snapshot of the categories, so editing them is always allowed. Status includes `survey` counters and unsearched-room details.

## Acceptance

Independent expected behavior comes from the original user request and installed native game sources. Build the whole runtime before adding tests. Verify player workflows first in an isolated game cache and display, then integration and category/persistence contracts where gameplay does not cover boundaries. Multiplayer requires a disposable normal server with client mod activation and no LootBox server files. No test may modify the user's running game, personal saves or Workshop sources. Portable and native-interpreter tests alone cannot establish working multiplayer gameplay.

## Cooperative input and stopped checkpoints

Native reports temporary admission contention as `busy` and a settled incidental interruption as `yielded`. Jobs waits for arbitrary queued player work without a list of food or action classes. Aiming and attacking/shoving pause until explicit Resume. Deliberate run/sprint input stops with a resumable checkpoint; Escape pauses unless that press closes a window, menu or text entry. Death and sleep stop active work. Queue cleanup removes only owned work and preserves foreign action references and ordering. Completion by immutable item ID and type takes precedence over classifying an interrupted action, including a short observation grace for a stopped multiplayer transfer.

Jobs retains an operation and its completion continuation because dispatch may already have advanced a source, walking, or cleanup cursor. Pause settles owned native work and retains a private stopped checkpoint. Cancel settles owned native work and discards execution callbacks and any paused checkpoint without deleting carried items or personal-gear recovery receipts. Cancel supersedes a pending pause without issuing native cleanup twice. Resume restores that same execution and applies any already observed completed mutation once. It re-resolves anchored endpoints and returns to original floor locations before new work. Container continuations consume current resolved endpoints rather than handles captured before a long pause. Temporary vehicle restoration context outlives a yielded native action so cancelling during another player action still restores the original seat or outside state afterward.

Resume retires bin review tokens, keeps approvals, and tries refused cans again. A sent deletion stays under native observation; it is never blindly replayed. Stopped checkpoints are transient to the current player session. A new job sets one previous job aside; a second displaces it.

All framed windows resize through native grips and update existing controls in place. Layout does not rebuild pages, persist drafts, replace controls, clear focus, or reset scrolling during a drag. Minimum heights account for native font row height and leave room for the resize grip. Shared list rows retain full plain text and separate item, decision/status, and description segments; item/status columns reserve space so long names cannot hide the decision.

Settings is one scrolling list of grouped switches. A click saves that preference at once and redraws the list without moving it; a failed save shows its reason below the list, beside Write a snapshot to the log. Rows have a fixed height, a title or a title with a one-line description cut to fit, and the shared renderer draws each line only where it fits whole inside the list.

## Exact stack selection

Select amount is a top-level conditional item action. Hit testing uses screen-space UI mouse coordinates, independently of camera zoom. SelectionRequest captures the native pane and inventory, group name, player, original member references, and displayed maximum. Confirmation refreshes and resolves that same group; a reused floor inventory cannot adopt unrelated same-name items. It validates a positive decimal integer against the current count and never reduces the amount silently. No item transfer, job, or inventory mutation is submitted.

Partial selections use the native item marker for exact child references and expand real rows or cells. Standard panes keep their native collapsed state; active grid views delegate opening and reveal coordinates to the existing view API when available, without requiring that addon. Native saveSelection writes an item marker for the representative; the owned restore adapter normalizes that marker for expanded multi-item groups so a refresh cannot select the header and make a later drag take the whole group. Full counts preserve the native collapsed group marker. One unselected sentinel child remains rendered after a partial amount to prevent native header completion from counting an unrelated following row.

The native 50-row limit is a shared class field. Four instance wrappers scope it to the owning pane during selectIndex, onMouseDown, restoreSelection, and renderdetails. Pane state retains the largest requested partial render limit. An external base and nested call depth isolate another pane's default or own limit and restore the prior class value on success or error. Every known inventory/loot pane is attached through the existing refresh event, including default-limit panes. Nothing persists a global limit.

The numeric prompt owns its entry, button callbacks, validation, and color picker. It releases only its focused entry before removal and removes the separately registered picker. Cancel never applies a selection. Disconnect and main-menu cleanup close owned prompts. The workflow is mouse-only because native controller updates replace mouse selection.

## Personal hauling and deletion outcomes

A work registry joins source identity, native item ID and full type. Emptying a can settles the selected work that was already in it as cleared, while successful delivery increments moved; done is their sum. Capacity failures defer finitely and retry only after successful unloading or gear changes advance the capacity revision. Sent trash commands remain observed through cancellation until their snapshot disappears or the native timeout expires.

Carry planning uses the actual remaining selected work and all observed bag candidates within the reviewed source scope. Native worn-location and clothing-extra metadata owns compatibility. Occupied equipment is retained. Native conversion observes the replacement ID and exact direct contents, then migrates private labels and work references. Capacity-only preparation failures may retry once at a changed capacity revision.

Recovery writes a separate verified Store receipt before staging and before each native intent. It reserves an ordinary static container and exact personal root/descendant identities. Operations bypass ordinary selection protection only when they match that durable intent. Preparation excludes the selected heavy source ancestry and preflights both heavy admission and raw bag readmission in a feasible restore order. Recovery streams and approaches the recorded endpoint before movement, then observes each transfer and original-slot restoration. Failure retains the receipt, and a failed manual recovery retains the original paused checkpoint while requiring recovery before ordinary Resume or new job admission. Cancelling recovery discards that continuation, not the receipt; recovering afterward cannot revive a cancelled job.

Review display uses App identity and monotonic review generation, separate from short-lived approval tokens. Automatic polling uses existing sessions only. The job key creates the in-world session, so a saved gear-recovery receipt shows on the strip as Gear left in storage, with Fetch my gear.

## Matching transfers and optional reviews

Inventory routing remains the Smart Load policy. An explicit carried container instead transfers directly into that container without a temporary inventory leg or unrelated bag preparation. The matching destination has portable mode displayed or nearby and requires inventory sources with nested bags. At preview, World captures native matching-type aliases and container anchors from the current visible loot targets. Private target snapshots stay out of the public preview request and saved setup documents. Native sorting orders the detached source list once, and Jobs retains one ordered carried batch across bags. Floor routes capture their original drop point.

`Matching` adds buttons beside the stock controls they extend: Put Away and Condense before Transfer All, Match Displayed and Match Nearby after the stock matching transfers, and Smart Take All and Smart Take Matching after the loot window's Take All and Take Same Type, for containers and the floor. A button shows only while it has something to do, rechecked at most every 0.7 seconds; hover highlights are cached briefly and a click always reads afresh. Without its stock peer a button is not added. `matchingTransferButtons` governs Match and Smart Take, and `putAwayButton` and `condenseButton` their own buttons, independently of the right-click menus.

Review preferences default off and preserve explicit saved values. App.reviewPreference owns the mapping from transfer, cleanup or bin review to its preference and uses review when stored preferences cannot be read. App.start applies the default bin policy unless a per-job option overrides it. A job that starts without a review shows nothing until it lasts or needs the player; then the job strip appears, or only when it needs the player if the strip is turned off. Ticking Don't ask in a review turns that preference off after the job has started; a failed write does not stop the job.

Explicit shortcut intent distinguishes Condense, matching modes, and Smart Take modes; Smart Take of one container also checks which. Pressing a toolbar button again resumes the job it started, current or set aside, or leaves it running; only when there is none does it plan afresh. A different action sets the current job aside once its plan is known to be startable. Nothing auto-starts from a stale click. Stop on the job strip ends a job for good. The job key (F7 by default) shows or hides the strip while there is a job, and opens the planner otherwise. The planner and right-click menus always plan afresh, even while a job is paused. Starting such a plan sets the paused job aside; a newer press of the same toolbar button drops it instead unless it is carrying something, and Put Away, which is for certain items, starts afresh. A plan made while a job exists belongs to a second runner, so resuming the job does not discard it. Active native work and required personal-gear recovery still block new planning.
